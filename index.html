The Map

The two faint rings circling Grand Central come from a 'breadth-first search' out of the central hub. Distance is measured as the fewest clicks it takes to reach each page. The thin connectors repeat the search from every page, to every other page. Edge (or 'link') thickness is proportionate to the share of shortest routes passing through it (called 'edge betweenness'). Brandes' algorithm computes the solution to this Betweenness Centrality problem with one search per page instead of comparing every pair.

How to read it

  • Grand Central, in the center, is the hub. Each dotted ring is one more click away.
  • A colored line is one site and each dot on it is a page. Dashed orange dots are under construction; dotted grey dots are planned.
  • Letters beside a page show which other lines it links to. Fainter, thicker connectors carry more of the shortest routes.
How it is computed

Rings come from a breadth-first search out of Grand Central: a page's distance is the fewest clicks needed to reach it. Connector thickness is edge betweenness, the share of shortest routes passing through a link, calculated with Brandes' algorithm.
